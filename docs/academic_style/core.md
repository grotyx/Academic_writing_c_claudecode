ACADEMIC WRITING MODE (manuwright): applies to every manuscript sentence you draft or rewrite.
Targets are measured from 33 openly licensed papers in JAMA Surgery, JAMA Network Open, Lancet, BMJ
and Nature (2019-2022, written before generative AI; docs/academic_style/reference_profile.json).
- Register: plain, exact, declarative. Mean sentence length is about 22-24 words in Methods and
  Results and 27-28 in the Introduction and Discussion; about 1 sentence in 9 runs past 40 words
  and 1 in 20 past 50. One idea per sentence; one topic per paragraph, stated first.
- Plain verbs win: "showed" outnumbers "demonstrated" about 5:1, and "used" appears 345 times
  against "utilized" once. Write "used", "showed", "found", "was associated with".
- Voice follows the section: Methods mostly passive (63% of sentences), Results mostly active
  (24% passive), Introduction and Discussion about 30% passive. "We" is normal (about 1 per 120
  words in the Discussion).
- Claims in proportion to evidence. Observational designs: "was associated with", "suggests",
  "may". Causal verbs (reduces, prevents, improves) only for randomized evidence. Hedges cluster in
  the Discussion (about 1 per 115 words) and are rare in Methods and Results.
- Clinical findings are the grammatical subject, not the test. Same term for the same concept;
  abbreviations defined once. Estimate and 95% CI before *p*; exact *p*; never *p* = 0.000.
- Never in these journals (0 occurrences in 179,000 words): delve, underscore, showcase, leverage,
  intricate, realm, paramount, seamless, holistic, meticulous, "it is worth noting", "it is
  important to note", "plays a crucial role", "shed light on". Rare (under 1 per 50,000 words):
  crucial, pivotal, interestingly, remarkably. No contractions (0), no bold, exclamation marks or
  rhetorical questions in running text; no em dashes as punctuation.
- Bad: "Notably, our findings underscore the pivotal role of early mobilization, highlighting its
  importance." Good: "Early mobilization was associated with a shorter hospital stay (mean
  difference -1.2 days; 95% CI -2.0 to -0.4)."
- Bad: "Interestingly, the novel technique demonstrated remarkable efficacy." Good: "Leg pain
  decreased more after endoscopic decompression than after microscopic decompression."
- Before you send a section, check: (1) no fact, number, citation or claim was added or dropped
  that the plan, results/*.csv and evidence.md do not support; (2) the first sentence of each
  paragraph states its point; (3) no banned word or tail clause above; (4) every "significant"
  carries an estimate, CI or *p*; (5) the last sentence does not restate the paragraph.
- Before drafting or rewriting a section, load its card: `manuwright style card <section>`
  (title, abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, conclusion): moves, phrasebank,
  model paragraphs, measured targets, and your own corpus style when learned.
