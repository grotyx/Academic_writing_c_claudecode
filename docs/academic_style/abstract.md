# Abstract

## Moves (structured; use the journal's headings)
1. Background or Objective (1-2 sentences): the gap and the aim.
2. Methods (2-4 sentences): design, setting and dates, participants, intervention or exposure,
   primary outcome with time point, main analysis.
3. Results (3-5 sentences): number analysed; primary outcome per group with the effect estimate,
   95% CI and *p*; the most important secondary outcome; serious adverse events.
4. Conclusions (1-2 sentences): the answer to the aim, worded no more strongly than the design
   allows; the implication.
Length: the journal limit, usually 250-300 words. Keywords: 3-6 MeSH terms.

## Rules
- Written last; every number identical to the text and tables.
- No citations, no abbreviations that are not defined here, no "data not shown".
- Results give numbers, not adjectives. Conclusions do not introduce anything absent from Results.

## Phrasebank
- "This retrospective cohort study included ... patients who underwent ... at ... between ...
  and ...", "The primary outcome was ...".
- "... occurred in ...% after X and ...% after Y (hazard ratio ...; 95% CI ... to ...)."
- "X was associated with a lower risk of ... than Y. A randomized trial is needed to confirm ..."

## Model paragraph
Background: Endoscopic decompression for lumbar spinal stenosis preserves more of the posterior
elements than microscopic decompression, but whether it reduces reoperation is unknown.
Methods: This retrospective cohort study included 862 patients aged 50 years or older who
underwent single-level decompression at two university hospitals between 2015 and 2018. The
primary outcome was reoperation within 5 years, analysed with a Fine-Gray model adjusted for age,
sex, body mass index and diabetes.
Results: Reoperation occurred in 31 of 402 patients (7.7%) after endoscopic and 58 of 460 (12.6%)
after microscopic decompression (subdistribution hazard ratio 0.62; 95% CI 0.40 to 0.96).
Reoperations for instability accounted for most of the difference.
Conclusions: Endoscopic decompression was associated with fewer reoperations at 5 years,
mainly for instability. A randomized comparison with long follow-up is needed.
