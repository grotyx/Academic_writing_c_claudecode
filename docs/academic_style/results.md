# Results

## Moves
1. Participants: how many were screened, included and analysed (refer to the flow figure);
   baseline characteristics by group, in one or two sentences that point to Table 1.
2. Primary outcome: the estimate in each group, the between-group difference or ratio with its
   95% CI, then *p*.
3. Secondary outcomes: in the order the Methods listed them; details in tables.
4. Additional analyses: subgroup and sensitivity analyses, labelled as such.
5. Adverse events and complications.

## Rules
- Report, do not interpret: no "suggests", "may explain", "because", "clinically meaningful".
- No citations and no methods; every analysis reported here was described in Methods.
- The text carries the key numbers; tables carry the rest. Never repeat a table row in prose.
- Past tense; findings are the subject ("Reoperation was less frequent ...").
- Report nonsignificant primary results explicitly; do not list nonsignificant secondary
  *p* values in the text.

## Phrasebank
- "Of the ... patients screened, ... were included (Fig. 1).", "Baseline characteristics were
  similar between groups (Table 1)."
- "The 5-year cumulative incidence of ... was ...% after X and ...% after Y (hazard ratio ...;
  95% CI ... to ...; *p* = ...)."
- "... did not differ between groups (Table 2).", "The results were consistent in the
  sensitivity analysis that ...", "No ... occurred in either group."

## Avoid -> prefer
- "Interestingly, group A showed a remarkable improvement" -> "Leg pain decreased more in group
  A (mean difference ...; 95% CI ...)."
- "There was a statistically significant difference (p < 0.05)" -> give the estimate, CI and
  exact *p*.
- "As shown in Table 2, the mean age was ..., the mean BMI was ..." -> "Baseline characteristics
  are shown in Table 1."

## Model paragraphs
Of the 1,214 patients who underwent decompression during the study period, 862 met the
eligibility criteria; 402 had endoscopic and 460 had microscopic decompression (Fig. 1).
Patients in the endoscopic group were younger and more often had single-level disease (Table 1).

Within 5 years, 31 patients (7.7%) in the endoscopic group and 58 (12.6%) in the microscopic
group underwent reoperation. After adjustment, endoscopic decompression was associated with a
lower risk of reoperation (subdistribution hazard ratio 0.62; 95% CI 0.40 to 0.96; *p* = 0.032).
Reoperation for instability accounted for most of the difference (Table 2). The estimate was
similar when patients with diabetes were excluded (Supplementary Table 2).
