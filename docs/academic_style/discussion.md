# Discussion

## Moves
1. Principal finding (one paragraph, 2-4 sentences): answer the study question in words, without
   repeating the numbers from Results.
2. Comparison with previous studies (1-3 paragraphs): where the findings agree and where they
   differ, with the likely reasons (population, design, definitions, follow-up). Cite each study.
3. Explanation (one paragraph): plausible mechanisms, hedged in proportion to the evidence.
4. Strengths and limitations (one or two paragraphs): specific limitations, the direction in which
   each could bias the result, and what was done to address it.
5. Implications: what a clinician or researcher should do differently, and the specific study
   that would answer the remaining question.
Length: 4-6 paragraphs, about 900-1,500 words, unless the journal sets less.

## Rules
- Interpretation in the present tense; specific findings and prior studies in the past tense.
- Hedging matches the design: randomized evidence may support "reduces"; observational evidence
  supports "was associated with" and "suggests". One hedge per claim, not "may possibly suggest".
- Do not restate background from the Introduction or numbers from Results.
- Limitations are specific ("Reoperations at other hospitals were not captured, which would
  underestimate ..."), never a generic list ("retrospective design, small sample").
- "Further studies are needed" only with the design that is needed.

## Phrasebank
- Principal finding: "In this cohort, X was associated with a lower risk of ...", "These findings
  indicate that ...", "The main finding of this study was that ..."
- Comparison: "This finding is consistent with ...", "In contrast, ... reported ..., which may
  reflect ...", "The difference between ... and our results may be explained by ..."
- Explanation: "One possible explanation is that ...", "This interpretation is supported by ..."
- Limitations: "This study has limitations.", "First, ... Second, ...", "Because ..., the
  estimate may overstate/understate ...", "Residual confounding by ... cannot be excluded."
- Implications: "These results support ...", "A randomized trial with ... as the primary outcome
  would establish whether ..."

## Avoid -> prefer
- "Our study clearly demonstrates the superiority of X" -> "X was associated with fewer ..."
- "This highlights the crucial importance of ..." -> state the specific consequence.
- "This study has several limitations, including its retrospective design and small sample
  size." -> name each limitation, its likely bias and how it was mitigated.
- "Further research is warranted." -> "A trial randomizing ... would test whether ..."

## Model paragraphs
In this cohort of 862 patients, endoscopic decompression was associated with a lower 5-year risk
of reoperation than microscopic decompression, and the difference was confined to reoperations
for instability. The finding supports the premise that limiting resection of the posterior
elements preserves segmental stability.

Previous comparisons found no difference in reoperation [EVID:author_year]. Those studies followed
patients for 2 years, whereas most reoperations for instability in our cohort occurred after the
second year. Short follow-up may therefore explain the discrepancy.

This study has limitations. First, allocation was not random; surgeons chose the technique, and
patients with more complex stenosis more often had microscopic decompression. Adjustment for
the number of levels and for spondylolisthesis reduced, but cannot have removed, this
confounding, which would favour the endoscopic group. Second, reoperations were identified from
claims data, so the indication for reoperation relied on diagnostic codes.
